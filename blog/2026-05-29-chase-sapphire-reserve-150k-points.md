## What it is

Chase is offering a **150,000 Ultimate Rewards points** welcome bonus on the Sapphire Reserve after spending **$6,000 in the first three months**. No end date has been announced, but the offer launched April 30, 2026, and elevated bonuses like this one historically disappear within 4–8 weeks without warning.

This is the largest public welcome offer the Sapphire Reserve has ever had. The previous record was 125,000 points.

## Why it's worth it

Per TPG's May 2026 valuations, 150,000 Ultimate Rewards points are worth **$3,075**. Via the Chase Travel portal with Points Boost (look for the rocket symbol on eligible rates), you can squeeze up to **2 cents per point** out of the bonus — meaning it can cover up to $3,000 in flights and hotels booked through Chase.

What makes Ultimate Rewards different from most bank points: they transfer to **14 airline and hotel partners at 1:1**. That's where the real leverage is. Some examples of what 150,000 points can do:

- **United MileagePlus:** 2–3 round-trip business class flights to Europe, depending on the route
- **World of Hyatt:** 10–15 nights at category 3–4 properties globally
- **Air France/KLM Flying Blue:** 3–4 business class flights to Paris on promo award rates
- **Singapore KrisFlyer:** 1 first-class round trip to Asia when award space opens

The card itself also carries a **$300 annual travel credit** that applies automatically to any travel purchase, **Priority Pass Select** lounge access (unlimited visits plus two guests), and a **$100 Global Entry/TSA PreCheck credit** every four years. For a traveler who flies a handful of times a year, those benefits net out most of the annual fee before the points are even part of the math.

## The catch

**The annual fee is $795.** That's among the highest of any consumer travel card. If you won't use the $300 travel credit, lounge access, and trip protection benefits regularly, the fee doesn't make sense. This is not a card to leave in a drawer.

**Eligibility is strict.** Chase's 5/24 rule applies — if you've opened five or more new credit cards in the past 24 months, expect a decline regardless of the bonus. More importantly: if you've previously received a Sapphire Reserve welcome bonus, you almost certainly won't qualify, even if you cancelled the card years ago. Same applies if you received a Sapphire Preferred bonus in the past two years.

**The spend requirement is $2,000/month for three months.** That's manageable for many people — put rent, utilities, groceries, and regular bills on the card — but it needs to come from real spending. Manufactured spend risks account closure and a bonus clawback.

**No end date means no safe waiting period.** Chase pulls these elevated offers without advance notice.

## Where to get it and when it expires

Apply directly at **chase.com** under the Sapphire Reserve card page. The offer is live on the standard public application.

No deadline is published. Based on Chase's history with elevated Sapphire offers — the last major elevated bonus ran approximately six weeks — this one is already a month in. It could end this week or run another month. There's no signal.

First-year math for anyone weighing the fee: the $300 travel credit posts to any travel purchase, bringing the effective first-year fee to $495. Subtract the $100 Global Entry credit and you're looking at a ~$395 net cost in year one while collecting $3,075 in points. That's a clear trade for anyone who travels at all.

Year two is where you decide if the card earns its keep on its own benefits. Most heavy travelers say yes. Occasional travelers usually don't renew, which is fine — the bonus is yours either way.
