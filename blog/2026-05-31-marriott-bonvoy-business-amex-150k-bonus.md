## What it is

The Marriott Bonvoy Business American Express card is currently offering 150,000 bonus points plus a $125 statement credit after spending $8,000 on purchases within the first six months of opening. This is the highest points offer the card has ever had.

The annual fee is $125. The welcome offer includes a $125 statement credit. In practice, that means your first year's annual fee is covered — you're getting 150,000 points effectively at no net cost, provided you can hit the spend.

The offer expires July 15, 2026. That's 46 days from today.

## Why it's worth it

Marriott Bonvoy points are valued at around 0.9 cents each by most analysts, which puts 150,000 points at roughly $1,350 in hotel value. Here's what that actually books:

**3 nights at a JW Marriott or Westin in a secondary market.** Solid 4-star Marriott properties — JW Marriott Austin, Westin Copley Place Boston, Le Méridien Nashville — run 40,000–50,000 points per night. Three nights at one of those uses 120,000–150,000 points, which is essentially your entire welcome bonus applied to real travel.

**5–7 nights at a standard Marriott or Courtyard.** Mid-tier properties in most US cities run 15,000–30,000 points per night. If you're staying at Marriotts for work trips anyway, the bonus covers 5–10 nights free.

**One night at something genuinely nice.** The Ritz-Carlton Kyoto runs 80,000–100,000 points. The W Maldives is around 50,000. The St. Regis Florence is roughly 85,000. You could put a serious portion of this bonus toward a single night at a property you'd otherwise skip.

Beyond the welcome bonus, ongoing card perks include:

- **Annual Free Night Award** (up to 35,000 points, capped) every year after your card renewal month — worth $200–$350 at a typical redemption
- **Marriott Bonvoy Gold Elite status** for the calendar year, which gets you room upgrades, 25% bonus points on stays, and late checkout
- **15 Elite Night Credits annually**, which stack toward higher status tiers if you're already accumulating nights at Marriott properties

## The catch

**This is a business card.** You don't need an LLC, an EIN, or a brick-and-mortar business. Any form of self-employment qualifies you as a sole proprietor: freelancing, consulting, selling on eBay, driving for a rideshare company, photography side work. Your legal name is the business name; your Social Security number is the tax ID. American Express does not audit the nature of your business — they check your creditworthiness.

If you have no business income or activity of any kind, this card isn't available to you.

**$8,000 in 6 months is a real threshold.** That's about $1,333/month. For business owners, this is usually straightforward — route normal business expenses through the card. For personal spenders, it requires planning: big purchases, prepaid subscriptions, or splitting spend with a partner who puts their expenses on your account.

**Marriott points don't travel well.** Unlike Chase Ultimate Rewards or Amex Membership Rewards, Marriott points are primarily useful within the Marriott ecosystem. You can transfer them to airline miles, but the conversion rate is 3:1 (three Marriott points = one airline mile), which destroys the value. If you want flexibility across hotels and airlines, this isn't your best card.

**The $125 statement credit takes time to appear.** It typically posts 2–3 billing cycles after you meet the full $8,000 minimum spend, not immediately.

**Check your bonus eligibility.** American Express's terms state the welcome bonus is not available to applicants who have previously received a new card member bonus on this specific card. If you've had the Marriott Bonvoy Business Amex before and earned a welcome bonus, you likely won't earn another one.

## Where to get it and when it expires

Apply directly at AmericanExpress.com — search "Marriott Bonvoy Business American Express Card" or navigate through Marriott.com's credit card landing page. The application is online; most decisions are instant.

**The offer expires July 15, 2026.** No extension has been announced. A separate offer earlier this year — 5 Free Night Award certificates worth up to 50,000 points each — ran briefly and expired May 13. The points version is the only offer currently on the table.

If you're eligible and can hit the spend, the math is simple: $1,350+ in points, annual fee offset by the statement credit, ongoing free night certificate, and 46 days to apply.
