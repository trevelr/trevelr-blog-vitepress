## Why it's worth it

These are repositioning sailings — Celebrity and Princess move ships from their European summer deployments back to Florida and the Caribbean ahead of winter season. They fill the ship by pricing sharply below what the same vessel charges on peak sailings.

The Celebrity Xcel's inside cabin runs $89/night. The same class of Celebrity Edge-class ship costs roughly $180–190/night on a comparable Caribbean itinerary at this time of year. You're getting the same ship, same crew, same restaurants and bars — for about half the price.

The Sky Princess at $61/night is even more stark. Princess's own $399 Caribbean deal posted here earlier this month worked out to $57/night on a 7-night itinerary, and that required a specific limited promotion. The Sky Princess transatlantic is open availability on princess.com right now, no promotion code needed.

The itinerary isn't just filler. The Celebrity Xcel routing stops in Valencia and Cádiz in southern Spain, then Lisbon, then Ponta Delgada in the Azores — mid-Atlantic volcanic islands that most people never have a reason to visit otherwise — then Bermuda, then Miami. Eight ports over 16 nights. The Sky Princess adds France and Portugal before the crossing.

If you've ever thought about doing an ocean crossing on a ship, this is what that experience looks like at an accessible price point.

## The catch

**This is one-way.** You arrive in Florida, not back where you started. That means you need to either plan your trip so you're already in Europe when you board — fly home from Fort Lauderdale or Port Canaveral after arrival — or fly to the departure port first and factor a return flight home into the total cost. A round-trip home from Florida adds $200–$400 in flights for most US cities, which still leaves the math well in your favor, but it's real money that changes the headline price.

**It's 15–16 nights.** Once you're deep in the Atlantic, you're not getting off early. If your schedule has any flexibility doubt, sort that out before booking.

**Sea days.** After the European ports, you'll spend somewhere between 6–9 consecutive days at sea crossing the Atlantic. Some people find a stretch of uninterrupted ocean days genuinely restorative. Others find that many days without a port stop frustrating. Know which you are before committing.

**Southampton logistics (Sky Princess).** Southampton is an hour by train from London Waterloo — not complicated, but it is a separate journey. If you're flying into Heathrow, budget 2–3 hours to get from the airport to the port.

**WiFi if you're working remotely.** Celebrity ships have Starlink, which means functional ocean connectivity. Some Princess ships are still running legacy satellite systems that struggle mid-crossing. If you're considering this as a remote-work transatlantic — check which connectivity package is available before booking the Princess option.
