Kyrgyzstan's mountain season is four months. Specifically: June through September, when the high passes stay open and the alpine meadows aren't buried under snow. Within those four months, two different things are happening this year.

July is when you go to Altyn Arashan, the Ala-Kul lake, and the trekking routes east of Karakol — before the Issyk-Kul hotels fill up and the Bishkek-to-lake corridor jams. August 31 is when the World Nomad Games open at Bishkek Arena, and September 1 through 6 is when they move to the lake's north shore: Cholpon-Ata, Kyrchyn Ethno Village. Thousands of athletes, dozens of countries, craft markets, kok-boru, falconry.

The Tian Shan Mountains landed on the New York Times 52 Places list this year. Tourism doubled between 2023 and 2025. The erosion on the Ala-Kul trail is already visible.

This is not a place that's still undiscovered. It is still excellent. The question is which part of the summer you want to be there for.

## South Shore, Not North Shore

Every travel piece about Issyk-Kul defaults to the north shore — Cholpon-Ata, Bosteri, the Soviet-era beach complexes that now cater to Kazakh vacationers and, increasingly, to tourists following those travel lists. The north shore is fine. It's also where the Nomad Games happen, which means late August through early September is the wrong time to be looking for a quiet hotel room there.

The south shore is the one worth protecting. Less developed, quieter, and home to Skazka Canyon: a 1.5-kilometer loop through red and orange sandstone formations above the lake. "Skazka" is Russian for fairy tale. The canyon entrance fee is 50 Kyrgyz som — roughly 60 cents. There are no inflatable water slides on the south shore.

![Skazka Canyon's red formations on Issyk-Kul's south shore](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/07/Skazka_Canyon,_Kyrgyzstan_(29685812067).jpg)

The town of Karakol sits 12 kilometers from the lake's eastern end and serves as the base for almost everything worth doing in the region. It's not charming. It has working guesthouses, a functional produce market, taxi drivers who know the mountain roads, and direct access to the Terskey Alatau range.

## Altyn Arashan

South of Karakol, the Ak-Suu River valley runs into the mountains. Drive or hike 12 kilometers to the road's end, then continue 7 kilometers on trail to Altyn Arashan — a cluster of guesthouses and yurt camps at 2,500 meters, built around a set of natural hot springs. Above the camp, the trail branches to Ala-Kul: a turquoise high-altitude lake at 3,532 meters, ringed by glaciers.

This is the trek that justified every "go to Kyrgyzstan" article written in the past three years. The lake is real. Book the Altyn Arashan guesthouse before you leave Karakol — not at the trailhead, and not by assuming you'll figure it out when you arrive.

![The Tian Shan glaciers above Altyn Arashan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fd/Glacier_in_Tian_Shan_AB.jpg)

## About the Games

The World Nomad Games are not a tourist event. They're a genuine international competition in traditional nomadic sports: kok-boru (mounted polo played with a goat carcass), horseback archery, falconry, kurosh wrestling, Toguz Korgool. The sixth edition runs August 31 through September 6, with the opening ceremony at Bishkek Arena and the bulk of the competitions at the Kyrchyn Ethno Village and Cholpon-Ata sports complex.

![Kok-boru, the traditional equestrian game at the World Nomad Games](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e6/Kok-boru,_Kyrgyzstan.jpg)

If the games are the reason you're going, go. The Kyrchyn Ethno Village — archery, falconry, craft markets, food from nomadic traditions across Central Asia — is worth a full day. Book accommodation in Karakol or Bishkek rather than Cholpon-Ata; the north shore hotels are essentially sold out and priced accordingly. Expect the lake corridor to be slow.

If the games are not the reason you're going, July is better than August. The trails are fully accessible, the guesthouses have room, and Ala-Kul is not yet on a booking queue.

## The Honest Caveat

National parks around Karakol raised maintenance fees this year, largely because the erosion and litter on popular routes are now visible rather than theoretical. Tour operators remain unregulated — equipment safety inspections are not required. Trekking above 3,000 meters requires travel insurance that explicitly covers mountain search and rescue. Many standard policies don't. Check yours before you leave, not when you're at altitude.

Prices are up roughly 15–20% from two years ago. The country is no longer a budget outlier. It's merely affordable. That's still better than where the trajectory points.
