## What it is

Expedia and Hotels.com are running parallel summer sales right now, both closing on **July 13, 2026**. The stated discount is up to 40% off select properties, with travel dates covering June 2 through December 15, 2026. Featured properties span domestic and international — Honolulu (Prince Waikiki), Cancun (Fiesta Americana Condesa All Inclusive), Portland (Sentinel Hotel), and Bermuda (Cambridge Beaches Resort & Spa), among thousands of others.

Both platforms are part of Expedia Group, so the inventory overlaps significantly. You don't need to check both; pick whichever loyalty program matters to you (Hotels.com uses its own rewards program, Expedia runs One Key points).

## Why it's worth it

The combination here is the thing. The travel window extends through **December 15** — this isn't a summer-travel-only sale. If you're thinking about fall or holiday travel and haven't booked yet, you can lock in hotels now at reduced rates and still cancel if plans change.

That's the second part worth paying attention to: **free cancellation is available as a filter option**. Most aggressively discounted hotel rates are non-refundable — you get the lower price in exchange for committing. This sale includes free cancellation inventory, which means you can book a discounted rate and walk away with no penalty if you change your mind before the cancellation window.

The use case: you're vaguely planning an October trip to Charleston, a Thanksgiving week in New Orleans, or a December long weekend somewhere. You're not committed yet. Locking in a discounted, refundable rate now is meaningfully better than watching prices drift up over the next few months while you decide. If you go, you paid less. If you don't, you cancel.

## The catch

**"Up to 40%" is the ceiling, not the typical outcome.** Most hotels in this sale will show 10–20% off. Some show nothing meaningful at all. The biggest discounts cluster around specific properties on specific dates. You have to search and evaluate, not assume every result in the sale is significantly discounted.

OTA platforms also routinely inflate their displayed "regular rate" — the reference price from which they calculate the markdown. A "40% off" badge can reflect a starting price that most travelers never paid. Before committing to any property, open Google Hotels for the same dates and compare. If Hotels.com or Expedia is pricing meaningfully lower than what you see elsewhere, the discount is real. If it's roughly the same, the sale badge is marketing.

**Filter for free cancellation explicitly.** Not all properties in this sale offer it. The platform will mix refundable and non-refundable rates in the same results by default. Go to Hotels.com or Expedia, search your destination and dates, then apply the "Free Cancellation" filter before you start evaluating discounts. Without the filter, you may book a non-refundable rate and lose the flexibility that makes this sale worth using.

**Peak summer availability is thin.** July and early August dates at popular beach resorts and major city hotels are likely to have already absorbed most of the best discounted inventory. The sale is more useful for fall and December bookings where availability is broader and prices are more flexible. If you're targeting a specific August property, check it now — don't assume it'll be available at the end of the booking window.

## Where to book and when it expires

Book at **hotels.com** or **expedia.com**. No promo code required — sale pricing loads automatically for qualifying properties. The booking window runs through **July 13, 2026, at 11:59 p.m. property local time**. Travel can extend through December 15 as long as the booking is made before the deadline.

Two search tips that will help:

1. Apply the **"Free Cancellation"** filter first, then look at prices.
2. Sort by **"Price (low to high)"** and look for properties showing a visible strike-through original price — that's where the actual sale inventory tends to concentrate.

The deal is real. Whether it's exceptional for your specific trip depends on your destination and dates, which is why you need to verify the pricing yourself before calling it a win.
