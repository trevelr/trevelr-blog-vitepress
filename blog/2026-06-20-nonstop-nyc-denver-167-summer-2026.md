## What it is

Round-trip nonstop flights between New York (LGA) or Newark (EWR) and Denver (DEN) are currently running at **$167** on Southwest and United Airlines. Available travel dates cluster through July and August: July 7–8, 14–15, 17–18, 22, 24–25, 27–29 and August 7–8, 14–15, 21–22, 28–29.

Both airports are in play — Southwest operates from both LGA and EWR, while United is primarily out of their Newark hub. The pricing appears across multiple aggregators (Google Flights, KAYAK, Slickdeals) and isn't tied to a promo code or member-only rate.

## Why it's worth it

Southwest's average fare on the New York–Denver route typically runs $314–$324. At $167, that's roughly 48% below average — for a nonstop flight to one of the most outdoor-accessible cities in the country. Denver is the gateway to Rocky Mountain National Park, Breckenridge, Vail, and most of the Colorado high country. In summer, those areas are at peak hiking and rafting season.

To put the pricing in context: this is roughly what you'd pay for a domestic route half the distance. New York to Chicago runs $180–$220 on average. You're getting more flight for the same money.

The deal gets even better if you're flying carry-on only (more on that below).

## How to book it

**Book Southwest.** Even though United shows up at a similar price, Southwest's fare terms are meaningfully better here. Southwest Basic includes a carry-on bag and a personal item, and allows free date changes with no penalty (you just get the fare difference as travel credit if the new fare is cheaper).

United Basic Economy, by contrast, allows only a personal item — no carry-on — and doesn't permit changes or refunds of any kind.

Go to southwest.com directly. Search LGA or EWR to DEN for your target dates. The $167 fares appear on the midweek dates listed above — Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday departures are where most of the inventory is. Avoid the July 4th weekend entirely (July 3–6): that's a pricing blackout with fares typically running $80–$100 higher.

Booking takes about 10 minutes, no account required.

## The catch

**Southwest no longer does bags fly free.** As of April 9, 2026, checked bag fees are $45 for the first bag and $55 for the second — on every fare class except Choice Extra, and for non-status members without a Rapid Rewards credit card. That's a significant shift from where Southwest was even a year ago.

If you're checking a bag each way, your effective round-trip cost is $167 + $90 = $257. Still cheaper than the $314 average, but the gap narrows from 48% savings to around 18%. For a three-night weekend trip where you can make a carry-on work, this deal is genuinely excellent. For a two-week trip to Colorado requiring luggage, run the numbers against alternatives before committing.

Also worth noting: Denver is a mile high, and the outdoor itineraries that make this trip worthwhile tend to generate gear. Hiking boots, layers, a rain jacket — this adds up. Plan your packing accordingly or build the bag fee into your math.

## How long it'll last

This isn't a countdown-timer flash sale. There's no code, no expiration date listed anywhere. These are Southwest's lowest inventory fare class seats priced into the summer schedule — and when those seats fill, the price goes up, and you can't get it back.

The good news: with 15+ specific dates spread across July and August, not all of them will sell out at once. The bad news: peak July dates (particularly the 14th–18th window, which catches a lot of vacation weeks) will move faster than the August dates.

If you're targeting July travel, book this week. If you're flexible and can take an August trip, you likely have more time — but not infinite time. There's no reward for waiting on a deal this specific.
