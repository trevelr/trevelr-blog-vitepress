On August 12, Spain gets its first total solar eclipse over the mainland since 1905. The path enters via Galicia around 7:30pm local time, crosses the meseta at sunset, and exits over the Balearics — which means every location on this list is a low-sun, west-facing gamble, not a guarantee. So this list isn't ranked by fame or duration alone. It's ranked by the thing that actually matters: your realistic odds of seeing totality at all, weighed against whether the trip is worth taking if the clouds win.

## #10 — Bilbao

**The default pick for people who want to combine the eclipse with a Guggenheim weekend, and that's exactly the wrong logic.** Bilbao sits right at the edge of the path, which caps totality at roughly 30 seconds — barely enough to get your glasses off before it's over. Basque coastal weather doesn't help either.

*Caveat:* If you're already committed to Bilbao for other reasons, fine, but don't build a trip around it. You'll get a nice partial eclipse and a very short story.

## #9 — A Coruña

**The eclipse's first landfall in Spain, and the most historically loaded spot on this list.** The shadow crosses the Tower of Hercules — a working Roman lighthouse — about 74 seconds after the coast goes total, which is a genuinely great setting for it. But Galicia averages close to 60% cloud cover on August evenings, and on past 12ths of August, the sky would only have cooperated about half the time.

*Caveat:* Book flexible and have a car. If the coast socks in, you're driving inland, not standing on the cliff you booked a hotel for.

## #8 — Oviedo

**The longest totality anywhere on mainland Spain, at roughly 1 minute 49 seconds.** Asturias also happens to be the cloudiest stretch of the entire eclipse track — around 55–60% average cover, with clear-sky odds sitting near 38%. Cider houses and the Picos de Europa make it a worthwhile trip regardless, which is the only reason it's not lower.

*Caveat:* This is the highest-reward, lowest-odds pick on the list. Go if you're chasing the longest possible totality and can live with a coin flip.

## #7 — S'Arenal, Mallorca

**The best weather odds in the entire country — roughly 75% clear — and the most photogenic setup, watching the shadow race out over the Mediterranean.** The problem isn't the sky. It's that the path exits Spain here, near the edge of the band, so totality is shorter than it is inland, and the island has become the most expensive place on this list by a wide margin — one hotel in Valldemossa jumped from €138 to €518 a night for that one date.

*Caveat:* Book now or don't bother. Tour operators occupying the best cliffside properties sold out months ago.

## #6 — Logroño

**The practical, undersold choice.** La Rioja gets roughly 1 minute 22 seconds of totality with decent inland weather odds, and nobody's writing panicked headlines about hotel prices here yet. Pair it with a wine region that's genuinely worth the detour on its own.

*Caveat:* It's not the longest totality or the driest sky on the list — it's just reliably fine, which counts for something eleven days out.

## #5 — Palencia

**Was the insider's secret spot. Isn't anymore.** Palencia sits dead-center on the meseta with 1 minute 42 seconds of totality and some of the best inland weather odds in Spain — which is exactly why word got out. Rooms here have been quoted north of €1,095 a night, more than ten times a normal August rate.

*Caveat:* The eclipse math is excellent. The price math is a cautionary tale about being right too early on the internet.

## #4 — Zaragoza

**The least romantic pick and the most functional one.** A major rail hub with real infrastructure, 1 minute 25 seconds of totality, and the dry Ebro valley climate working in your favor. If you want to book a normal hotel through a normal website without a bidding war, this is your city.

*Caveat:* It won't be the story you tell at dinner parties. It'll be the trip that actually happened, which is underrated.

## #3 — León

**Better weather odds than the coast, longer totality than the capital cities south of it — 1 minute 44 seconds — and a Camino de Santiago cathedral town that's worth walking around regardless of what the sky does.** Still bookable at something close to a normal rate, for now.

*Caveat:* That won't last through August 12. If León is the plan, lock it in this week, not next.

## #2 — Burgos

**The consensus pick, and for once the consensus is right.** Near the centerline, 1 minute 44 seconds of totality, and meseta weather odds — roughly 35% fully clear plus another 40% partly cloudy but still viewable — that beat almost everywhere else on the mainland. El Cid is buried in the cathedral, which is a strange but fitting detail for a city about to have its main historical moment in over a century.

*Caveat:* Everyone else did this math too. Standard hotel rooms are already quoted above £500 a night for the eclipse window.

## #1 — Teruel

**The name nobody puts at the top of these lists, and the numbers say that's a mistake.** Teruel sits near the centerline with 1 minute 35 seconds of totality, but the real case is the sky: this date has historically been cloudy only about 20% of the time here — the driest odds anywhere on the Spanish track, coast or meseta. Throw in UNESCO Mudéjar towers like the Torre de El Salvador and the fact that almost nobody has thought to book here yet, and you get the rare eclipse trip where you can still find a hotel room that isn't extortion.

*Caveat:* It's genuinely less famous than Burgos or Mallorca, and if the trip doesn't work out you won't have a five-star hotel story to console yourself with. You will, statistically, have the eclipse.
