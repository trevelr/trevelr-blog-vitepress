Prague just landed at #3 on U.S. News' list of the world's best places to visit in 2026, which is the exact moment to ask what else is out there. This list is ranked by one criterion: how much of what makes Prague great (a real old town, serious beer, cheap and easy transit, history you can touch) you get without the crowds. Number one is going to annoy you.

## 10. Tirana, Albania

**The loud one.** Bunk'Art turns a Cold War bunker into a museum of the Hoxha regime, and the Blloku district, once reserved for the party elite, is now the city's bar-and-café quarter. The Dajti Ekspres cable car puts you on a mountain in about fifteen minutes. *Caveat:* the traffic is chaotic and the old town is thin; this is a city of neighborhoods, not a postcard.

## 9. Kraków, Poland

**The safe pick, and it knows it.** Kazimierz is still the best-preserved Jewish quarter in the region, the Schindler's Factory museum is genuinely well done, and Nowa Huta, a communist planned district, is a strange afternoon that most visitors skip. *Caveat:* the main square is as crowded as Prague's, so this is "instead of Prague" in geography only.

## 8. Košice, Slovakia

**The easternmost Gothic cathedral in Europe.** St. Elisabeth's anchors a long, pedestrianized main street lined with cafés and a singing fountain, and the city did a stint as a European Capital of Culture in 2013 that left real infrastructure behind. *Caveat:* it's far east, and you'll want a car or a long train ride from anywhere else.

## 7. Wrocław, Poland

**Island-hopping in a city of bridges.** Ostrów Tumski, the cathedral island, is gas-lit at dusk, and Centennial Hall is a UNESCO-listed concrete landmark. Hunting the city's small bronze dwarf statues is a surprisingly good way to see the whole place. *Caveat:* the dwarf thing is a little twee by day three.

## 6. Plovdiv, Bulgaria

**Roman theatre, still in use.** Plovdiv claims to be among the oldest continuously inhabited cities in Europe, and you can sit in a Roman amphitheatre in the old town that still hosts concerts. The Kapana creative district, redone around its 2019 European Capital of Culture year, handles the evenings. *Caveat:* getting there without a car from Western Europe means a stop in Sofia.

## 5. Timișoara, Romania

**First in a very specific way.** This was the first city on the continent to get electric street lighting, in 1884, and it's where the 1989 revolution began. Its three squares are painted in Habsburg pastels, and it held the European Capital of Culture title in 2023. *Caveat:* restoration is uneven, and some facades are still waiting for their turn.

## 4. Sofia, Bulgaria

**A capital with a mountain attached.** Alexander Nevsky Cathedral is the postcard, but the real draw is Vitosha, a ski-and-hiking mountain you can reach on city transit. Meals cost a fraction of Prague prices. *Caveat:* Soviet-era concrete dominates large parts of town, and you have to like that.

## 3. Sarajevo, Bosnia and Herzegovina

**Ottoman market on one side, Austro-Hungarian boulevards on the other.** Walk from the Sebilj fountain in Baščaršija a few minutes west and the architecture changes entirely. The Tunnel of Hope museum is essential for understanding the 1990s siege, and the ćevapi are the best-value lunch on this list. *Caveat:* it's a heavy history, and it's not a city to treat as a backdrop.

## 2. Vilnius, Lithuania

**The baroque old town nobody talks about.** It's one of the largest surviving old towns in the region, with some forty churches packed into it, and the self-declared artists' republic of Užupis has its own constitution on a wall. *Caveat:* winter is long and dark, so go between May and September.

## 1. Brno, Czechia

**Prague's honest little sibling, 2.5 hours away.** Brno has Villa Tugendhat, Mies van der Rohe's UNESCO-listed house, and a Capuchin crypt, and under St. James Church sits the Brno Ossuary, holding the remains of more than 50,000 people, the second-largest in Europe after the Paris catacombs. The Špilberk fortress sits above the old town, the beer is the same Czech beer for less, and direct trains from Prague (RegioJet, Leo Express, České dráhy) compete on price. *Caveat:* it has no single skyline moment, and Tugendhat tickets sell out within minutes, so book ahead.

Yes, Brno at #1 is the controversial pick: it's in the same country as Prague, which feels like cheating. But the criterion is Prague's pull minus Prague's crowds, and no other city on this list delivers that ratio so cleanly. Fight me.

*Rankings reflect my own weighting, informed by [U.S. News' 2026 list](https://travel.usnews.com/rankings/worlds-best-vacations/) and 2026 trending-destination roundups showing Eastern Europe's surge.*
