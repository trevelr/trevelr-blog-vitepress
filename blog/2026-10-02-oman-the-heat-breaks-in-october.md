Every autumn-travel report this year says the same thing: people want cooler weather, fewer queues, and places that still feel like themselves. Most of them then recommend Scotland.

Oman has a better answer, and the reason is thermometer-simple. From roughly October to April, Muscat stops being a place you survive and becomes a place you can walk around. The window opens this month.

## What Oman Actually Is Right Now

Visitor numbers are climbing. The country counted 752,474 arrivals in January and February 2026, up 12.6% on the same stretch of 2025, with the UAE, India and Germany leading. The sharper number is in the mountains: Jabal Akhdar logged 101,608 visitors in the first half of 2026 against 89,780 a year earlier. That's 13.2% more, and it's heading into 2027 as the Gulf Tourism Capital.

Read that carefully. A mountain plateau that most people can't place on a map is about to get a title and a marketing budget. Whatever "undiscovered" means, you have a short window.

## Mutrah, First

Start on the Corniche in Mutrah, Muscat's old harbor district. Go late afternoon. The waterfront promenade runs along the harbor with the Hajar mountains stacked behind it, and in October the evening air finally cooperates.

![Mutrah at night, from the waterfront](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/12/Intermission_in_Mutrah.jpg)

Mutrah Souq is the point of the neighborhood. It's a tangle of lanes selling frankincense, silver, textiles and spices under a modern timber roof, which sounds like a downgrade and mostly isn't. The chaos survived the renovation. Buy frankincense here, since this is the country that built its history on it, and haggle without apology.

Eat at Bait Al Luban, in a restored stone building at the northern end of the Corniche. The balcony looks over the harbor and the mountains. Then go cheaper: Haji Ali Juice Centre is the kind of place you stop for a fresh juice and end up staying twenty minutes.

![Mutrah and its port, seen from above the fish market](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c3/Oman-Muscat-Muttrah-21-Marina.JPG)

Fit in the Bait Al Zubair museum and Al Jalali and Al Mirani forts, which overlook the old city and are quick to cover.

## Then Leave Muscat

Jabal Akhdar is why this post exists. It's a green plateau in the Hajar range with terraced orchards of pomegranates, apricots and roses, and a climate that sits well below the coast's. The rose harvest, which drives the plateau's April festival, is a real agricultural operation: more than 6,500 rose trees, up to 30,000 liters of rosewater a year. You won't catch the harvest in October. You will catch the terraces without the spring crowds.

Nizwa is the other obvious day out, and the fort and the old town reward an early start.

## Why Now

The timing argument is boring and strong. The heat breaks. Jabal Akhdar's infrastructure is being built up for 2027. Right now you get the improvements without the crowds that the title will bring. In a year, this is a different trip.

## The Honest Caveat

Oman is spread out. Muscat, Nizwa and the mountain are separate trips with real driving between them, so renting a car is close to mandatory, and a long weekend won't cover it. Mutrah is also not a secret. The Corniche and the souq fill with tour groups at midday, which is why the late-afternoon advice is not decorative.

And the 2027 buildup cuts both ways. Growth of 13% is good for guesthouses. It's less good for the quiet you were hoping to find.

I haven't stood on that Corniche. The numbers, the names and the seasons are from the reporting. The verdict, though, is mine: if you were going to chase cool weather north, check the south first.
