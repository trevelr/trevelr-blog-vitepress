On November 23, a Swedish private operator called Snälltåget is sending a sleeper train from Malmö to Narvik, Norway. Total distance: 2,156 km. It crosses the Arctic Circle, stops in Kiruna and Abisko, and ends at a fjord. The return leaves Narvik on November 27. One northbound trip. One southbound trip. Then it's gone.

That's the hook, and it's a real one. This isn't a timetable you can plan around next year. It's a special operation, and the schedule is still marked preliminary because of track works.

## What it actually is

Stockholm to the far north is usually a flight or a long daytime run. This is the slow version. The provisional timetable has the train leaving Malmö at 13:15, passing Stockholm at 18:55, crossing the Arctic Circle around 10:00 the next morning, reaching Kiruna at 13:00 and Abisko at 14:45. Narvik follows later that afternoon. Treat every one of those times as a draft.

Cheapest private sleeper compartment for Interrail pass holders: SEK 2,499. Regular fares weren't published when I looked. The ticket covers the train, not a bed at the other end, so lodging is yours to sort out. Compartments sleep one to six people, and a return booking in a private compartment gets 20% off.

## Kiruna: a town being moved

Kiruna sits on top of the world's largest underground iron-ore mine, run by LKAB. As the mine went deeper, cracks showed up in roads and buildings. The answer was to move the town center east, a project underway since 2004, with roughly 3,000 homes and 6,000 people affected.

In August 2025 they moved the church. Kiruna Church, designed by Gustaf Wickman and consecrated in 1912, was voted Sweden's best building from before 1950 in a 2001 poll. It rolled about five kilometers across town to the new center.

![Kiruna Church, now relocated to the new town center](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fa/Kiruna_kyrka_August_2017_02.jpg)

Go and look at it. Not because it's pretty, though it is, but because you won't see many places where a town's survival is an engineering schedule. Some Sámi reindeer herders object to LKAB's plans for a new mine because of what it would do to migration routes. That argument is unfinished, and the new center is the visible edge of it.

## Abisko: the point of the trip

Abisko's reputation as an aurora spot is old enough to have infrastructure. The Aurora Sky Station on Mount Njullá opened in 2007, reached by chairlift from the village, and it runs most days from November to April. The operator suggests staying at STF Abisko Turiststation, close to the national park. I'd book that now, before the train does the same thing to everyone else's plans.

![Aurora in Abisko National Park](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/aa/Aurora_in_Abisko_National_Park.jpg)

The train reaches Abisko in the afternoon. In late November that means dark is close. You step off, check in, and you're in position for the first night. That's the actual advantage over flying into Kiruna: you arrive already adjusted.

## Narvik: the end of the line

The last stretch is the Ofoten Line, 43 km of single track from the Swedish border down to the port. It exists to carry ore. Up to twelve freight trains a day haul it to Narvik, whose LKAB port handles around 25 million tonnes a year. You're riding a passenger service on an industrial line, and the views from it are a side effect.

## Why now

Because the window is a single week. Because Interrail holders get the cheapest berth. Because Kiruna is changing every year, and the old and new town centers won't coexist forever.

## The honest caveat

Auroras aren't guaranteed. Cloud doesn't care about your ticket. You'll spend roughly 27 hours on a train to get a chance at a sky, not a promise of one, and the schedule could shift. Check Snälltåget's own booking page before you spend anything. I couldn't reach it from my end, so the fares and times above come from press coverage of the announcement.

Also: it's a long way to go for a short stay. Between the outbound train on the 23rd and the return on the 27th, you get about two nights on the ground. Pick Abisko or Narvik, not both.
