Skyscanner's 2026 trending list put Bilbao at +97% in search growth. Everyone will tell you why: the Guggenheim. I'd bet against that. The museum has been there since 1997. Nothing about a titanium building changed this year. What changed is what travelers say they want, and Skyscanner's own report is blunt about it: 35% of global travelers plan to visit or shop at a local grocery store on their next trip. People are planning around lunch now.

Bilbao is very good at lunch.

## What it actually is

A river city that spent the twentieth century making steel and the last thirty years deciding what to do after. The Nervión runs through it, Frank Gehry's museum sits on the bank under the red arch of the Salve bridge, and the old town, the Casco Viejo, is a tight grid of streets on the opposite side that most visitors reach by accident on the way to a bar.

![The Nervión estuary at Bilbao](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/12/La_ría_de_Bilbao_(rio_Nervión).jpg)

## Four places worth your time

**Mercado de la Ribera.** One guide calls it Europe's largest covered food market, at more than 10,000 square metres. I can't verify the superlative, but the scale is the point. This is a working market, with fish and produce stalls, and pintxos bars alongside the actual shopping. It's the "supermarket safari" trend with better lighting. Go before noon, buy something you can't identify, ask what it is.

![Inside the Mercado de la Ribera](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f0/Bilbao_-_Mercado_de_la_Ribera_4.jpg)

**Plaza Nueva.** Laid out in 1821 and ringed with arcades, it hosts a cluster of bars including Gure Toki (Plaza Nueva 12). Order the soft-boiled egg with mushrooms, potato and Idiazábal cheese cream. Eat it standing. That's the whole method: one bar, one or two pintxos, one drink, leave. Repeat four times. The Casco Viejo has over a hundred bars within walking distance, so you will never run out of next.

![Plaza Nueva in Bilbao's Casco Viejo](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/31/Plaza_Nueva-Bilbao.jpg)

**Bar El Globo (Diputación 8).** The signature is *gratinado de txangurro*, baked spider crab in a creamy gratin. It's rich, it's small, it's what you came for. Then walk it off.

**Funicular de Artxanda.** Running since 1915, it takes about three minutes to climb the hill above the city. The view back over the estuary and the Casco Viejo is the best free orientation Bilbao offers. Do it in the late afternoon, after lunch has settled.

![The Artxanda funicular](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e7/Funicular_artxanda.jpg)

Worth a side trip: Azkuna Zentroa, a former wine warehouse reworked by Philippe Starck into a cultural center. It's what a post-industrial city does with a big empty building when it has already built the famous one.

## Why now

Because the demand finally matches what the city already had. Skyscanner's report describes travelers choosing places that feel like an iconic destination without the crowds, and slower trips built around food rather than checklists. Bilbao fits both, and it fits them at Basque pintxo prices. The museum gets you to book the flight. The bars and the market are why you extend the trip.

## The honest caveat

Pintxos are cheap per piece and expensive per evening. Guides put them anywhere from roughly €2.50 to €8 each depending on the bar, and a proper crawl is never one piece. Budget accordingly. Also: weekends and holidays are when the Guggenheim queues build, so book a timed entry if you're going. And a 97% search jump is search growth, not arrival numbers. I couldn't find 2026 visitor counts, so I won't pretend to have them.
